Pocket Library Spine studies · external review

Eight approaches · one smallest-cover problem

The spine is the whole cover, compressed.

A shelf spine is two centimetres of colour, one vertical line of type, and a memory of the front cover. These prototypes ask what belonging looks like at that size: what to keep, what to crop, and what to rebuild when the original art is buried under print.

The question

Can a book’s own cover become a convincing, legible spine without flattening it into a thumbnail — and which technique is honest enough to ship? The studies run from photography-led crops, through text removal and generative reconstruction, to a measured design system and an independent reference implementation.

Reproducibility

One random downloadable book, the same book in every study.

The shared book

The Hound of the Baskervilles

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1902. Chosen once, deterministically, from a documented list of downloadable Standard Ebooks titles. The real 1400 × 2100 cover is downloaded and kept with the test; every study in this suite is measured against it.
