Pocket Library Spine studies · external review

Ten studies · one strict rule

The spine is the whole cover, compressed.

A shelf spine is two centimetres of colour, one vertical line of type, and a memory of the front cover. These prototypes ask what belonging looks like at that size: what to keep, what to crop, and what to rebuild when the original art is buried under print. The strict test at the bottom is simpler: a random downloadable book goes in, and the APK itself must make the spine.

The question

Can a book’s own cover become a convincing, legible spine without flattening it into a thumbnail — and which technique is honest enough to ship? The studies run from photography-led crops, through text removal and generative reconstruction, to a measured design system and an independent reference implementation.
