The cover, unprinted

Twenty spines made from the cover art alone. The printed title, author, quotes and badges are gone — either by using the original public-domain painting, or by masking the text and inpainting the cover. No text-masking bands; the only type left is the app’s own optional rail.

Research · what already exists

Nobody has solved this

We checked the main open-source removers and the places that might hold original artwork. The short version: PD classics can be sourced clean; modern trade covers cannot, as a rule. Full links and licence notes are in RESEARCH.md.

Removal · IOPaint + LaMa

IOPaint (Apache-2.0, now archived) and LaMa (Apache-2.0) are the practical CPU stack. LaMa is resolution-robust to ~2k; MAT is CC BY-NC and heavier; CTRNet/EraseNet/GaRNet are trained for scene text but their SCUT-EnsText data is non-commercial and the checkpoints are awkward.

Detection · RapidOCR

RapidOCR (Apache-2.0, ONNX, CPU) found 13 boxes on The Whisper Man and 17 on Rock Paper Scissors in ~5 s per 1303 × 2000 cover. It misses giant condensed display letters, so the mask still needs a human-drawn supplement.

Textless art · the honest state

The Book Cover Archive (credited final covers), I Need a Book Cover, AIGA 50 Books | 50 Covers, artist sites and cover reveals all show the published cover. We found no public prose-book archive that exposes flat/textless art; comic “textless covers” communities do, but that does not generalise.

PD art · automatable

Wikimedia Commons, The Met Open Access, Art Institute of Chicago, Rijksmuseum and Smithsonian Open Access have APIs and high-resolution files. This is the only source class that routinely works at scale.

Recommendation
  1. Classic / public domain: fetch the original painting or illustration; do not inpaint a typographic Standard Ebooks cover. This is what 15 of the 18 classics do here.
  2. Modern, with a genuine textless file: license it from the photographer/illustrator or publisher. Arcangel (the Whisper Man door/child photo, credited in a German edition to Hanka Steidle/Arcangel Images) and artist portfolios are licensable, not scrapeable; watermarked previews are not usable art.
  3. Modern, no textless source: OCR + mask + LaMa for modest type. Fix the spine crop to a clean region, or fall back to a typographic spine. Do not pretend a 70%-masked cover is clean art.
  4. Never silently keep printed cover type on an “art only” spine; if it remains, label it and design for it.

Design notes

What changed from v2

01 The art is now the source, not a masked cover

v2 put a 22% foot band over each printed title plate and a 9–14% cap over marketing copy. v3 removes those bands: there is no printed text left to hide. The spine is full-height art; the only optional overlay is the app’s title rail.

02 Classics come from the painting, not the typographic cover

Fifteen classics use the original public-domain painting from Wikimedia Commons. Three (Frankenstein, The Turn of the Screw, Alice) use the art above the Standard Ebooks plate because the exact source file was not available at high resolution. That is a crop, not an inpaint, and is labelled in the compare strip.

03 The modern covers show their scars

The Whisper Man is the pass: 38.8% of pixels masked (title, quote, two stickers, top line), 105 s on CPU, no residual OCR. Rock Paper Scissors is the failure: 70.4% masked, and LaMa turns a snow-forest photograph into grey mush. The spine crop is honest about it.

04 A rail is UI, and can be switched off

The rail is the app’s own title text, not the cover’s. It uses a smoked-glass channel on dark art and a warm vellum channel on light art, so title legibility does not depend on the cover. Toggle Art only to see the same shelves with zero type.

Still open:
  • Should the ingest pipeline reject a cover whose text mask exceeds, say, 35% of the art? Rock Paper Scissors says yes.
  • Is a narrow 33% slice of a textless painting still “the cover”, or does it become an arbitrary art crop? The original-painting route changes the composition more than v2’s cover crop did.
  • Can a lightweight super-resolution or texture pass make large-mask LaMa usable on high-frequency photography, without a diffusion model on CPU?
  • Do we need a per-title “clean region” map so the spine can avoid the masked area entirely instead of showing the repair?
