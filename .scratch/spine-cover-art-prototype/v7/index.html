Measuring type…

The pipeline

Original → mask → art → spine

The spine crop is the product. The full-cover art is evidence — including where it fails.

The finding

Commercial covers are hit and miss at 52 px

Removing type is not the hard part. The hard part is what is left underneath. A cover whose identity lives in a single object survives. A cover whose identity is its typography has nothing to reconstruct.

01

Flat black is easy

The Hobbit  and The Hunger Games  put type on flat black. A sampled dark-field fill removes it with no visible seam, and the red sun or gold pin does the recognition work.

02

Typography is not art

The Lord of the Rings  one-volume cover is mostly title and signature on black. The textless result is clean, and almost empty. It is a reject at spine scale: no object, no silhouette, no read.

03

Big type needs a strategy

Harry Potter  has a full-width title over painted sky. Large-mask LaMa smears. TELEA plus a detail crop saves the spine, but the full cover still has a flat magenta title field. Crop honestly; do not pass it off as a full reconstruction.

04

No diffusion editor was reachable

The built-in image tool returned an auth error. FLUX.1 Kontext and Qwen-Image-Edit Spaces hit the anonymous ZeroGPU quota. The prototype records that failure and uses the best local fallback: LaMa, TELEA and sampled clean fields.

Recommendation

Ship art only when the object survives.

Use Hybrid as the default for popular commercial covers: the paper label guarantees a readable title and the art window still carries the sun, pin or train. Use Editorial only when the art is strong enough to stand alone. For a cover like The Lord of the Rings, no titleless treatment is honest — fall back to type on cloth.
